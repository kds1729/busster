# BUSSter - Open Source Guitar Effects Routing

**Bus-based Effects Switching and Routing System**

An open source hardware project for programmable guitar effects pedal routing and scene switching.

---

## My Comments Before We Dive In

Years ago I was frustrated with my pedal situation. I spent a lot of time developing the BUSSter idea, 
and wrote up a whitepaper/propsal and sent it to my buddy Tom, who is intellectual property attorney 
and phenomenal guitar player. He looked it over and provided some feedback, which led me to a bit of 
a sobering conclusion that this project had some inherent problems, and would potentially be a lot of 
work.

My initial goal, in addition to having a pretty cool tool to organize my pedal combinations into 
groups of tones that could be mapped and recalled instantly was to create a prototype and rent a table 
at the NAMM show. I wasn't planning on a massive commercial campaign, I just wanted to be able to tell 
people I had a table at NAMM.

In addition to my NAMM motivation, I realized a long time ago that, when faced with more than about 
three pedals, in a live setting my ability to remember which combination of pedals produced which tones 
was more than I could manage. I wanted a way to pull up a song and be able to step through the various 
tones without having to think about what pedals needed to be turned on and when. 

Then I got a BOSS GT-1000. I love my BOSS GT-1000! More tones than I could play with in a lifetime. So 
I spent hours and hours mapping out tones, and creating custom variations of patches, moving them around 
and trying to create a tone taxonomy. I now have a pretty good working set of tones that I really like, 
and have been able to produce some pretty good recordings with.

But... at some point, the pedal addict resurfaced and I realized that, even though I have a good thing 
going with my GT-1000, there is something about having a big board of pedals at my feet that produces a 
certain type of euphoria that a single digital unit never could. I will never stop using my GT-1000, but 
each has its place. I had forgotten about this project years ago, but something that happened a little 
while ago reminded me of it.

Recently I woke up one morning and had the idea that I could use an AI agent to help develop the BUSSter 
idea. I use Claude Code extensively in other projects, so I pointed Claude at the whitepaper PDF and 
some perl and python code I had written years ago, and we got to work. The project structure, and almost 
all of the rest of this documentation was generated by Claude, based on my input. It should be pretty 
obvious reading the text that this is the case. I have read ever single markdown doc that was included 
with the initial release of this project, and there is a lot of what I would call "clumsy" language, 
and not all of the concepts are fully fleshed out. That is my way of saying if you see something that 
doesn't really make sense or looks a little off, that is Claude's fault not mine.

Now that the basic background stuff is out of the way, I am presenting this project to anybody who 
might have some interest in helping to develop it, for whatever reason. There will be a lot to learn, 
and some potentially not insignificant obstacles to negotiate. Hopefully at some point though, with 
help from a dedicated team of talented people, we can produce a working unit, and maybe get that table 
at the NAMM show to introduce it to the world.

## The Problem

Guitarists need to switch between potentially complex tone combinations during live performance. 
Current solutions either require tap-dancing across multiple pedals (error-prone) or cost a lot 
of money for commercial loop switchers that max out at 8-14 loops.

## The Solution

BUSSter provides **scene-based switching**: pre-program any combination of pedals as a "scene" 
and switch entire configurations with a single footswitch. No tap-dancing. No mistakes. Just 
press one button and instantly recall your tone.

### Example: One Song, Multiple Scenes

```
Song: "Smells Like Teen Spirit"
  Scene 1: Clean Intro    → [Comp: ON]  [Rest: OFF]
  Scene 2: Heavy Chorus   → [Comp: ON]  [Dist: ON]  [Chorus: ON]
  Scene 3: Clean Verse    → [Comp: ON]  [Octave: ON]
  Scene 4: Lead Solo      → [Comp: ON]  [OD: ON]  [Dist: ON]  [Delay: ON]
```

One button press per scene change. That's it.

---

## Key Features

- **Scalable**: Start with 3-5 pedals, scale to 20+ loops
- **Scene Management**: Store unlimited scenes, walk through them sequentially
- **Multiple Outputs**: Route to 4-8 different amplifiers or recording destinations
- **Silent Switching**: Solid-state analog multiplexers (no mechanical relay noise)
- **Fixed Pedal Order**: Pedals stay in musical order, each can be ON or OFF per scene
- **Open Source**: Fully customizable, community-driven improvements

### BUSSter Junior

A smaller, working sibling project lives in [junior/](junior/). Instead of
switching pedals, it switches patches on a multi-effects processor: dial up a
song on a floor unit and eight footswitches are loaded with the patches that
song needs. The laptop side (a Django app for the BOSS GT-1000) is complete;
the floor unit, a Raspberry Pi Pico, is next. See [junior/README.md](junior/README.md).

## Design Philosophy

**Start Simple, Improve Iteratively**

1. **Minimal Viable Product**: Prove the concept with minimal components
2. **Community Refinement**: Let builders discover real-world needs
3. **Multiple Build Levels**: Budget → Mid-level → High-end → Pro
4. **Organic Development**: Add buffers/gates/EQ only when needed

Different builders have different needs. The design accommodates all levels.

---

## Hardware Approach

- **74HC4051 analog multiplexers** for silent solid-state switching
- **Modular architecture** (add loops as needed)
- **True bypass** when pedals are OFF
- **No relays** (no mechanical noise)
- **Fast switching** (microseconds, not milliseconds)

## Success Criteria

BUSSter succeeds when:
1. Builders can create it from published designs
2. Audio quality is transparent (no tone degradation)
3. Switching is silent (no pops, clicks, or thuds)
4. Cost is reasonable ($200-500 for 10 loops vs $700-1500 commercial)
5. Community actively contributes improvements
6. It solves the real problem (makes live performance easier)
