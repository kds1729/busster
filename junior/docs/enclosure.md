# Floor-unit enclosure

The floor unit is a folded sheet-metal **hood** on a **wood base** with wood
end cheeks. The hood carries the ten footswitches, their LEDs and the display;
the base carries the electronics and the feet and leaves the underside open
for access. The design was worked out on 2026-11-15 from a notebook sketch and
is drawn to scale in [enclosure/enclosure.html](enclosure/enclosure.html),
generated by [enclosure/gen_layouts.py](enclosure/gen_layouts.py) (plain
Python, no dependencies; every position below comes from that script).

All sizes are in centimetres. Switch positions are hole centres.

## The hood

One sheet, four parallel bends, all 90 degrees or less, open underneath.

| Part | Size |
|---|---|
| Width | 48 |
| Back panel | 8 high |
| Flat band along the top of the back | 1.5 |
| Sloped face | 18.5 long at 19 degrees |
| Front edge | 2 high |
| Depth, back to front edge | 19 |
| Flat pattern (back + band + slope + front) | 48 x 30 |
| Top plate as seen from above (band + slope) | 48 x 20 |

The sketch's "option 2" replaces the two bends at the front edge with a single
1 cm radius. Whether that is cheaper is a question for the quote.

A closed one-piece wrap, with the sheet folding under the box to form a floor
with an access slot, was the first idea and was dropped: press-brake shops
cannot form a box that closes in on itself (SendCutSend says so explicitly),
and the ends of the box need closing anyway. The wood base and cheeks do both
jobs.

Material is undecided between 5052 aluminium at about 2.3 mm (0.090") and mild
steel at about 1.9 mm (14 gauge, needs powder coat). Steel is stiffer under
foot for the money. Pricing comes from an instant quote on an uploaded flat
pattern (SendCutSend, OSH Cut); neither shop publishes prices. Rough
expectation for a single hood: $70 to $120 in aluminium, $50 to $100 in steel,
plus $40 to $60 for powder coat, about 30% less each for two.

## Top-plate layout

Everything is measured on the flat top plate (48 x 20), from the left edge and
up from the front edge. The band occupies the top 1.5 cm of the plate.

**Footswitches 1 to 8** sit on a circular arc, radius 150 cm, that starts flat
at switch 1 and rises to switch 8. Every neighbouring pair is 6.08 cm apart
centre to centre. Six centimetres is the minimum for a player in boots; a
straight row at 6 cm needs the same 48 cm width, and the arc at 44 cm wide only
reaches 5.5 cm, so the width is what buys the clearance, and the arc is there
because it looks right.

| Switch | From left | Up from front |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3.0 | 4.0 |
| 2 | 9.1 | 4.1 |
| 3 | 15.1 | 4.5 |
| 4 | 21.2 | 5.1 |
| 5 | 27.2 | 6.0 |
| 6 | 33.2 | 7.1 |
| 7 | 39.1 | 8.4 |
| 8 | 45.0 | 10.0 |

**LEDs:** one 5 mm addressable RGB LED (WS2812 type) 2.5 cm above each
footswitch centre, eight in all, on one data line. The firmware sets the
colour: blue and green for the patch states, red on switch 8 when it is a page
switch. The song switches have no LEDs; the display shows the song.

**Song switches** (down, up) side by side at 3.5 and 9.5 from the left, level
with the centre of the display window, which puts them about 10 cm above
switch 1.

**Display window:** 11.0 x 6.7, left edge on the centre line of switch 3
(15.1 from the left), top edge 2.0 below the band. It is 1 mm larger than the
screen's active area on each side and inside the module outline, so the metal
hides the bezel. The window ends just past switch 4; the module behind the
plate clears the nearest LED hole by about 6 mm.

## Display and controller

A 5 inch 800x480 IPS panel on an integrated ESP32-S3 board, the **Sunton
ESP32-8048S050C** (capacitive touch version), which has an official
CircuitPython build. Screen 10.8 x 6.5; module outline about 12.1 x 7.6, to be
confirmed from the board drawing before anything is cut. The board is chosen
over the Makerfabs equivalent because the Sunton family has far wider community
support. Check the board revision on arrival: CircuitPython's setup differs
between revisions.

This replaces the Raspberry Pi Pico and the 128x64 OLED. The board has almost
no spare pins (one I2C bus and one or two GPIO), which is enough: the ten
switches go on an I2C port expander (MCP23017 class) and the eight LEDs on one
GPIO through the 74AHCT125 level shifter already in the parts list. MIDI OUT
uses the board's UART.

The panel size was settled by comparison: a 0.96" OLED is far too small to
read from standing height; a 4.58" 960x320 bar display (11.6 x 3.9) was too
short for more than one line; a 7" 800x480 (15.4 x 8.6) needed a 22 cm deep
plate and crowded the switches. The 5" is close to the 12 x 6 window in the
original sketch. A TN panel is not acceptable: the unit is viewed from standing
height at a steep angle and TN washes out.
