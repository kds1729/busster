# Floor-unit enclosure

The floor unit is one aluminium sheet with a single bend, an 8 cm **back**
and a sloped **control face**, resting on four plywood **wedge ribs** that
are glued and screwed to a plywood **base**, with a thin decorative hardwood
**front rail**. The sheet carries everything electrical:
the ten footswitches, their LEDs, the display, the jacks and the circuit
board. Four thumb screws hold it to the outer ribs, and when they are out the
whole chassis lifts off the wooden cradle in one piece.

The design was worked out on 2026-11-15 and 2026-11-16 from notebook sketches
and is drawn to scale in [enclosure/enclosure.html](enclosure/enclosure.html),
generated by [enclosure/gen_layouts.py](enclosure/gen_layouts.py) (plain
Python, no dependencies; every position below comes from that script).

All sizes are in centimetres. Switch positions are hole centres.

## Parts

| Part | Material | Size |
|---|---|---|
| Chassis | aluminium, one bend | sloped face 52 x 19.9, back 52 x 8; flat pattern 52 x 27.9, bent 107.5 degrees |
| Outer ribs (2) | 1/2" Baltic birch, three laser-cut layers glued up | 18.4 long, 8 high at the back, about 2.2 at the front end, 3.8 thick |
| Inner ribs (2) | 1/2" Baltic birch, one laser-cut layer | same profile, 1.3 thick |
| Front rail | hardwood, 1/4" x 1" stock strip, bought | 52 long, 0.6 thick, on the base under the front edge, 2 high at the front, top bevelled to the slope |
| Base | 1/2" Baltic birch, laser cut, sealed | 52 x 19 |

**Chassis.** Bare aluminium sanded to a matte finish; no paint or anodising,
which keeps it conductive and lets scuffs be rubbed out with a Scotch-Brite
pad. The ribs carry the load, so 1.5 to 2 mm is enough. The sheet is laser
cut flat with every hole in it and bent once, between the back and the
sloped face, at about 107.5 degrees; a single bend of any angle is routine
for a cut-and-bend shop. There is no seam anywhere on top, so a spill runs
down the slope and off the front edge. The back carries the power, USB and
MIDI OUT jacks and the circuit board bolts to its inside face next to them.

**Grounding.** The sheet is signal ground, bonded at exactly one point: a
crimped ring terminal with an internal-tooth star washer under the MIDI OUT
jack's mounting nut. Aluminium grows an insulating oxide within minutes of
sanding, so the star washer is what makes the contact; solder does not wet
aluminium at all. A dab of dielectric grease over the joint keeps the oxide
from creeping back.

**Ribs.** Each rib is a plain wedge, 8 high at the back sloping at 17.5
degrees to the front. The ribs are 18.4 long and stop behind the front rail,
about 2.2 high at that end; the rail continues the slope to the front edge.
There is no table saw in the shop, so the ribs are laser cut from 1/2" Baltic
birch in the same order as the aluminium: eight identical pieces, three glued
up for each outer rib and one for each inner rib. The outer ribs are flush
with the sheet's ends and are 3.8 thick so the thumb-screw inserts have plenty
of wood; their top back corner is chamfered to clear the inside radius of the
bend. The visible end faces can be sanded and oiled or faced with a thin
hardwood strip. The inner ribs are placed between switches and clear of the
display module; see the layout below. All ribs are glued and screwed to the
base from below. (If they are ever cut by hand instead, cut all of them
oversize, clamp the stack and plane the slopes as one gang, so they match
each other even if none is exact.)

**Hold-down.** Four flat-head knurled thumb screws (M4) through clearance
holes in the sloped face into brass threaded inserts in the sloped top of the
two outer ribs, 2.0 and 16.5 from the front edge. With a drill press the
insert holes are automatically perpendicular to the face. Flat heads rather
than stepped ones, so a boot cannot catch an edge. The back of the sheet
simply stands on the base against the ribs' back faces.

**Front rail.** Purely decorative, 1/4" stock. It stands on the base across
the full width under the front edge, with the ribs butting against its back
face, its top bevelled to the slope, glued to the base and the rib ends. It
occupies only the front 0.6 cm under the sheet, far clear of the switch
bodies, which start 3.1 cm from the front edge.

**Base.** 1/2" Baltic birch, laser cut in the same order with the feet holes
and the rib screw holes already in it, sealed on every face and edge; the ribs
and the chassis keep it from warping. Rubber feet underneath. Because the
chassis lifts off, the base needs no access opening.

## Layout of the sloped face

Everything is measured on the flat sloped face (52 x 19.9), from the left
edge and up from the front edge. The bend is at the back edge.

The face is 52 wide, not 48, because the outer ribs run under it: switches 1
and 8 need 5 cm from the ends to clear a 3.8 rib with 0.3 to spare. The
switch pattern itself is 42 wide, as before.

**Footswitches 1 to 8** sit on a circular arc, radius 150 cm, that starts flat
at switch 1 and rises to switch 8. Every neighbouring pair is 6.08 cm apart
centre to centre. Six centimetres is the minimum for a player in boots.

| Switch | From left | Up from front |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5.0 | 4.0 |
| 2 | 11.1 | 4.1 |
| 3 | 17.1 | 4.5 |
| 4 | 23.2 | 5.1 |
| 5 | 29.2 | 6.0 |
| 6 | 35.2 | 7.1 |
| 7 | 41.1 | 8.4 |
| 8 | 47.0 | 10.0 |

**LEDs:** one 5 mm addressable RGB LED (WS2812 type) 2.5 cm above each
footswitch centre, eight in all, on one data line. The firmware sets the
colour: blue and green for the patch states, red on switch 8 when it is a page
switch. The song switches have no LEDs; the display shows the song.

**Song switches** (down, up) side by side at 5.5 and 11.5 from the left, level
with the centre of the display window, which puts them about 10.5 cm above
switch 1.

**Display window:** 11.0 x 6.7, left edge on the centre line of switch 3
(17.1 from the left), top edge 2.0 below the bend. It is 1 mm larger than the
screen's active area on each side and inside the module outline, so the metal
hides the bezel. The module behind the face clears the nearest LED hole by
about 2 cm and the nearest rib by 1.5 cm.

**Circuit board.** The small custom board (I2C port expander for the switches,
74AHCT125 for the LED data line, MIDI OUT resistors, connectors) bolts to the
inside of the back next to the jacks, so it lifts out with the chassis.
Wiring to the switches and LEDs runs along the underside of the face and
through holes drilled in the inner ribs where it has to cross a bay.

## Back panel

Measured from the left edge as the player sees it, and up from the top of the
base. The back is 52 x 8. The display module hangs behind the middle bay,
right behind the bend, so nothing goes through the back there. The jacks are
grouped in the wide right-hand bay, on one line 4.0 up, and the circuit board
sits inside the back of the left-hand bay. Neither side was preferred; the
right bay is simply the wider one.

| Jack | From left | From right | Hole |
|---|---|---|---|
| DC power, 5.5 x 2.1 barrel | 45.8 | 6.2 | 8 mm (some jacks need 11) |
| USB-C panel-mount extension to the Sunton board's port | 41.3 | 10.7 | 10 x 4 cutout, two 3.2 mm screws 18 mm apart |
| MIDI OUT, 5-pin DIN | 36.3 | 15.7 | 16 mm, two 3.2 mm screws 22 mm apart |

Power is nearest the end so the wall-wart cable hangs clear; the MIDI jack,
with the biggest nut, has the most room, 3 cm from rib 3. The MIDI jack's
mounting nut is also the chassis ground point.

**Circuit board:** 5.0 x 4.0 with M3 mounting holes on a 4.4 x 3.4 pattern,
centred 8.4 from the left and 4.0 up, on standoffs inside the back. These
holes are placeholders in the cut file until the board is designed.

**Wiring holes:** a 2 cm hole bored through each inner rib, 3 cm from its
back face and 3.5 up, so the switch and LED wiring, the USB lead and the
display's connections can cross between bays.

## Display and controller

A 5 inch 800x480 IPS panel on an integrated ESP32-S3 board, the **Sunton
ESP32-8048S050C** (capacitive touch version), which has an official
CircuitPython build. Screen 10.8 x 6.5; module outline about 12.1 x 7.6, to be
confirmed from the board drawing before anything is cut. The board is chosen
over the Makerfabs equivalent because the Sunton family has far wider community
support. Check the board revision on arrival: CircuitPython's setup differs
between revisions.

This replaces the Raspberry Pi Pico and the 128x64 OLED. The board has almost
no spare pins (one I2C bus and one or two GPIO), which is enough: the ten
switches go on an I2C port expander (MCP23017 class) and the eight LEDs on one
GPIO through the 74AHCT125 level shifter already in the parts list. MIDI OUT
uses the board's UART.

The panel size was settled by comparison: a 0.96" OLED is far too small to
read from standing height; a 4.58" 960x320 bar display (11.6 x 3.9) was too
short for more than one line; a 7" 800x480 (15.4 x 8.6) needed a 22 cm deep
plate and crowded the switches. A TN panel is not acceptable: the unit is
viewed from standing height at a steep angle and TN washes out.

The flat chassis without the bend was $59.18, so the bend costs about $14. A
second chassis would be $50.27 each, and ribs fall to $5.93 each at ten. With
12 mm plywood the outer ribs come out 36 mm thick rather than 38, which
changes nothing in the layout. The base in 6.3 mm birch was $41.33, and
cutting it locally from a bought panel would cost about $15, but the laser-cut
12 mm base was chosen because it matches the ribs and the whole cradle looks
right.

A copper chassis (C110 half hard) was priced and rejected as too expensive,
though it would look beautiful: $451.55 at 1.6 mm, $344.51 at 1.0 mm, which
is too thin for the rib spans anyway.

Not in the quote: thumb screws and inserts, the front rail strip, feet,
sealer and oil, about $30 together.

## History

Three earlier metal designs were dropped. A closed one-piece wrap, whose sheet
folded under the box to make a floor with an access slot, because press-brake
shops cannot form a box that closes in on itself. A four-bend hood (back,
1.5 cm band, slope, front lip) on a wood base with end cheeks, because of the
same bending limits. Then a flat control panel plus a separate one-bend back
angle on the wooden ribs, because the seam between the two parts would always
let liquid in and needed a bonding wire to keep both parts grounded. Joining
those two parts into one sheet with one bend, and dropping the horizontal
band, removed both problems.
